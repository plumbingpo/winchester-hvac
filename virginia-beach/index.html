When Your System Fails in Virginia Beach

When your system quits, how fast help shows up matters more than anything else. For that reason, we keep our schedule flexible across the area and answer every call with a live person. Emergency calls typically see a technician within a day, and we treat no-heat and no-cool calls as top priority.

Common emergencies we handle include air conditioners blowing warm air, outdoor units that hum but won't start, furnaces that ignite and then shut down, breakers that trip whenever the system starts, iced-over evaporator coils, clogged condensate drains backing up into the furnace cabinet, and thermostats that have simply given up. Two common situations call for a quick step from you before our technician gets there. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: switch the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so the moving air thaws the coil, because running it frozen can flood the drain pan and hurt the compressor. The second is a furnace that short-cycles or produces a burning smell. Turn it off at the service switch and call us. Those few minutes can save you from a far bigger repair.

Keeping Your Virginia Beach HVAC System Running Longer

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep shrubs and grass trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and have a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still inexpensive.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular service, central air usually runs 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like higher energy bills, rooms that won't reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and is far less stressful than an emergency swap during the first heat wave of the year.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Take a weak capacitor: swapping it out is a quick, inexpensive repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a few miserable nights.
