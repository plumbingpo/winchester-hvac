Honest, Upfront Pricing

Nobody likes a surprise on the invoice. So Winchester HVAC quotes every Winchester job before the work begins, after a full diagnostic that covers the equipment's age, fuel type, and tonnage, refrigerant levels, coil and heat exchanger condition, and the ductwork feeding each room. You'll know what's wrong, what the fix involves, and exactly what it will cost before you say yes.

The trouble with HVAC failures is that many of them develop where you can't see them. A pinhole leak in the evaporator coil bleeds off refrigerant, the blower bearings wear down a little at a time, and a weak capacitor holds on until the first hot afternoon, when the compressor won't start. If your bills are climbing, airflow drops, or ice forms on the lines, have the system checked before a repair turns into a replacement.

How old is your system? Once an air conditioner or furnace passes the 12-year mark, it's worth thinking about the next one before the current one forces the issue. Emergency replacements happen on the worst days, in heat waves and cold snaps, when choices are limited and everyone is uncomfortable. Seasonal Tune-Ups

Tune-Ups That Keep Your System Running

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep your home comfortable year-round. Regular service heads off failures before they start, keeps coils and burners clean, and catches small faults while they're still inexpensive to fix. At Winchester HVAC, every plan is built around your home's specific equipment, with scheduled visits that cover coil cleaning, refrigerant charge verification, capacitor and contactor testing, burner and heat exchanger inspection, condensate drain clearing, filter replacement, and a static pressure check across the duct system.

Most homes do best with two visits a year: a spring tune-up for cooling and a fall tune-up for heating. The schedule flexes with the age of your equipment, how hard your household works it, and local conditions like dust and pollen, which clog condenser coils and push up energy use quickly.

The savings show up faster than most people expect. Clean coils move heat more efficiently, a correct refrigerant charge keeps the compressor from overworking, and a fresh filter protects the blower motor. Next to the cost of replacing a system even one year early, a maintenance plan is a bargain.

Sized for Your Home

Equipment Sized for Your Actual House

Proper sizing is one of the most overlooked parts of any install. Before recommending new equipment, Winchester HVAC measures the details that determine your home's heating and cooling load: square footage, insulation, window exposure, and duct layout. The result is a system that runs in long, efficient cycles, controls humidity, and holds every room at the temperature you set.

Choosing new equipment is about more than a SEER2 number. We'll compare furnace-and-AC setups with heat pumps, and ducted systems with ductless mini-splits, laying out operating costs, electrical and venting requirements, and the installation itself. Families with a growing household often do best with two-stage or variable-speed equipment, which holds more even temperatures from room to room and uses less energy. You choose what fits your home and budget.

Common Problems

Heating and Cooling Problems We Solve in Winchester

No cooling, a furnace that refuses to light, a frozen evaporator coil, a slow refrigerant leak: each of these problems has its own cause and calls for its own repair. Winchester HVAC tracks down the exact fault before replacing anything, whether it's a condenser that hums but won't start, a short-cycling furnace, a thermostat that keeps losing its program, a clogged condensate drain, or a mini-split throwing error codes.

The right repair depends entirely on the part that failed. On cooling systems, it's usually a capacitor, contactor, or fan motor. On furnaces, it tends to be an igniter, flame sensor, or control board. Leaky ductwork bleeding conditioned air into the attic is sealed and repaired, and refrigerant is recovered and recharged once the leak itself is repaired. A few of these problems are simply a nuisance, but a cracked heat exchanger or a clogged condensate drain can be a real safety or water-damage risk, and we treat it accordingly.
